What Handrail is

Handrail is a plan and a language, not an app

Handrail provides

The plan. One written format for a child’s rules, each with its reason.

The language. How a device or service receives a plan and reports back what it did.

The mark. A public test suite and a registry of what passed, run by a steward.

The guides. A Swedish default plan, community profiles, and an optional AI guide.

Who holds the plan

The family chooses. A Google family can make Family Link the main controller: it holds every plan and talks to the other products.

Or one app per device, each open, so it reports to the others or to a home hub.

Or a local controller that holds it all and tells the others what to do. The family decides how much they talk to each other.

Who follows it

Every certified device, console, app and platform. Each applies the plan on its own screens and reports back.

A vendor may not insist that a parent uses its app.

A vendor may not give another company’s app fewer controls than its own.

Handrail itself puts no app in front of parents. It publishes the standard, the test suite and free reference software that any vendor can build on.

A word on what Handrail is, because it is easy to picture it as one more app. It is not. It is four things. A written plan: one child’s rules, each with a reason. A language: how any device or service receives that plan and reports back honestly. A mark, with a public test, that says a product speaks the language. And guides: a Swedish default plan, community profiles and an optional AI guide. Who holds the plan? The family chooses. A Google family can make Family Link the main controller and let it talk to the Switch and the PlayStation. Another family keeps one app per device, each open to the others. A third runs a local controller that tells every device what to do. Who follows it? Every certified device and service, and none may shut the others out.
