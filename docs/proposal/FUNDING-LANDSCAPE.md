# Funding and commercial landscape for an open child-guidance standard

Research date: 21 October 2026. Sweden-based founder. UK English. Facts newest first.

## Summary

The money in this sector has moved from parental-control apps to age assurance. Aura raised 140m USD at a 1.6bn valuation (March 2025). k-ID raised 45m USD (June 2024) and now orchestrates age checks for Discord. Yoti grew revenue 62% to 29m GBP in 2025 on the back of the UK Online Safety Act. Pure parental-control consumer apps are small and slow: Qustodio is about 18m USD ARR inside Qoria (145m AUD group ARR, mostly schools), Bark last raised in 2022, Pinwheel and Gabb are bootstrapped or small. Nobody is funded to build an open standard for guided competence rather than gating.

The platform layer is the new fact. Apple's Declared Age Range API (global, February 2026) and Google's Play Age Signals API (global by end 2026) let a parent share a child's age band with apps, off by default, with analytics and advertising uses barred. These are the first vendor APIs that carry a parent-set child signal across to third parties. They carry only an age band, not a policy, a tag or a time budget. That gap is exactly where a Handrail-style standard would sit. Ofcom's first age-assurance report (July 2026) explicitly favours layered protection across devices, app stores and operating systems, which is the architectural argument for an interoperable standard. The EU age-verification mini-wallet (ready April 2026, due by end 2026) proves the privacy-preserving pattern governments will accept.

Grant routes for a Swedish founder: Vinnova Innovative Startups 2026 (up to 500,000 SEK, 100% funded, opens 12 October 2026, closes 12 January 2027) is the obvious first step, and the application window is open now. Almi Invest co-invests under 10m SEK. Norrsken is climate and health focused, not child safety. UK Safety Tech Challenge Fund rounds have been narrow (CSAM link sharing) and UK-registered only. eSafety Australia grants are for Australian NGOs. Horizon Europe has no open child-online-safety topic in 2026. Omidyar funds youth-led organisations, not startups.

Standards hosting: a W3C Community Group costs nothing and needs only a scope statement plus supporters. A Linux Foundation directed fund needs paying LF members and a six-figure budget. CSA (Matter) charges 7,500 to 112,500 USD a year, which is the model to imitate later, not first. ISRG runs three projects on about 7m USD a year from sponsors and donors. Signal spends about 38m USD a year and runs a deficit on donations. Both show that a foundation funded by donations works only after the product is indispensable.

Best fit: a Swedish AB holding the AI-guided parenting product, funded by Vinnova then Almi and angels, with the standard published free under a W3C Community Group so that platforms can join without a fee. Charge for the parent product and for conformance testing later. Weaknesses: the standard has no adoption lever until a regulator cites it; app vendors have never exposed a parental-control API; the age-assurance incumbents will say their age band is enough; and Sweden's commission wants platforms to verify age, which pushes money toward age checks and away from guidance.
