# Handrail annex: who to approach, and how to pay for it

*Supplementary paper to "Handrail: a floor and a path". Written 22 October 2026. Funding calls and political posts change often; check every date before acting on it. Quotes from Swedish sources are my own translations.*

## What this adds

The main paper names four asks and a 3.5 million EUR pilot. It does not say which organisations should hear each ask, or where the money could come from. This annex maps the Swedish, Nordic and EU bodies with a role. Where possible it uses what each one actually wrote in the consultation on SOU 2026:35. It then lists funding programmes that are open or due, with their size and rules. It ends with an order of approach for November 2026 to January 2027. One finding changes the main paper. Sweden's coordinator under the Digital Services Act is **PTS**, not Mediemyndigheten. Mediemyndigheten is the authority for the article on minors. The third finding matters most for Handrail. A European standards body looked at the standards bodies it studied and found none that explicitly addresses parental control. That gap is the opening for the standard.

## Two corrections to the main paper

**KIDS Act feedback date.** Two news reports give 26 November 2026 ([Brussels Times, 2 October 2026](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2343893/eu-kids-act-draft-sparks-widespread-feedback-push-to-reshape-digital-childhoods); [MLex, 1 October 2026](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/technology/articles/2533147/eu-kids-act-proposal-open-for-feedback-until-nov-26)). The Commission's own page says feedback is open "until 3 December 2026 (midnight Brussels time)" ([Commission, KIDS Act explained, updated 8 October 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/kids-act-explained), read 22 October 2026). The main paper's date is right. Aim to submit by 26 November anyway, so the earlier date cannot catch anyone out.

**Who enforces the DSA in Sweden.** The Digital Services Act (DSA) is the EU law that sets duties for online platforms. Each country names a Digital Services Coordinator (DSC), the single national contact for the law. By a government decision of 8 February 2024, **PTS** (Post- och telestyrelsen) was named as Sweden's DSC. PTS, Konsumentverket and Mediemyndigheten were all named "competent authorities", which means each one supervises some articles. Mediemyndigheten supervises several articles, including Article 28(1), the duty to protect minors. When a complaint concerns a platform based in another EU country, PTS assesses it and may pass it to the coordinator in that country ([PTS, annual DSA report, 29 September 2025](https://pts.se/contentassets/282a1426eba74963a9f059f9bd9e9166/verksamhetsrapport--art.55-dsa-for-2024.pdf)). The same report says PTS took part in all twelve meetings of the European Board for Digital Services in 2024. So, at EU level, PTS speaks for Sweden on the Board. Mediemyndigheten leads on Article 28.

## How to read the consultation

The consultation (remiss) on SOU 2026:35 was run by Socialdepartementet, unit for health and civil society, case S2026/01273. It went to 72 named bodies ([Regeringen, list of consultees, 16 June 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/remiss-instanser-for-sou-2026-35-en-aldersgrans-for-barns-tillgang-till-sociala-medier.pdf)). By 22 October, 55 responses were published ([Regeringen, remiss page, published 18 June 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/remisser/2026/06/remiss-sou-202635-en-aldersgrans-for-barns-tillgang-till-sociala-medier/)). Several invited bodies have no published response yet. They include Dataspelsbranschen (the games trade body), Meta, Snap, Svenskt Näringsliv, Svenska E-sportförbundet and Skärmtrygg barndom. The responses below are the best available guide to what each body already believes. A meeting that starts from a body's own words is easier to get.

## Children's and parents' organisations

- **Bris** runs the national child helpline, 116 111, and is the helpline partner in Sweden's Safer Internet Centre ([BIK, SIC Sweden profile, updated July 2026](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/sic/sweden)). The main paper already quotes its view that responsibility lies with the platforms. Bris also works with Telia on "Mobilkörkortet", a learning resource on mobile use, which gained a new section on AI in July 2026 ([Telia newsroom, 10 July 2026](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/telia/subjects/sociala-fraagor)). Ask Bris to review the never-blocked list and the child's right to contest a rule.
- **ECPAT Sverige** runs the hotline in the Safer Internet Centre. It rejects the proposed law. It warns that if the limit relies on parents' ability to enforce it, protection will be unequal. It also warns that a child who got round a ban may not dare tell an adult about abuse, because that would mean admitting it ([7 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/ecpat-sverige.pdf)). That second point matches Handrail's design, where the child is never punished for asking for help.
- **Stiftelsen Allmänna Barnhuset** (a children's foundation) takes no position until the final report. It notes that only children aged 10 to 18 had their views represented in the inquiry, so younger children were not heard ([8 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/stiftelsen-allmanna-barnhuset.pdf)). The pilot could fill that gap.
- **LSU** (the national council of Swedish children's and youth organisations) sits on the Safer Internet Centre's advisory board ([BIK, July 2026](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/sic/sweden)) and responded to the consultation ([LSU response, undated](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/lsu---landsradet-for-sveriges-barn--och-ungdomsorganisationer.pdf)). Ask it to recruit young reviewers for the child-facing screens.
- **Parents' groups.** Ki-DS (Bättre Digital Barndom) wants the limit set at 16 and agrees that parents should not be able to consent to earlier use ([Ki-DS response, undated](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/ki-ds--battre-digital-barndom.pdf)). Skärmtrygg barndom was invited but has no published response. These groups are likely to be sceptical of anything that sounds like a way round the limit. Approach them with the floor first: Handrail keeps the limit and adds a plan.
- **Friends** (anti-bullying) and **Rädda Barnen** (Save the Children Sweden). Neither was on the consultation list, and I found no published position from either on SOU 2026:35. Both are worth a short briefing. Friends' work on online bullying fits the "approved contacts" part of a plan.

## Internetstiftelsen

Internetstiftelsen (the Swedish Internet Foundation) runs the .se domain. Its research is the main paper's source on parents. It rejects the proposal in its current form. It sits on the Safer Internet Centre's advisory board ([BIK, July 2026](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/sic/sweden)). The foundation says that since 2004 it has funded "almost 400 projects" for about SEK 75 million in total ([Internetstiftelsen, undated page](https://internetstiftelsen.se/kunskap/for-samhallet/internetstiftelsen-stottar/)). That page does not say whether funding is still open. I found no open call in 2025 or 2026. (Unverified: that this funding ran as an open grant fund called Internetfonden, and that its old page no longer loads.) Treat it as a partner to ask, not a fund to apply to.

## Industry

- **TechSverige**, the tech companies' trade body, takes no position on the limit itself. It asks that platforms' ongoing work on parental controls, safe default settings and age-appropriate features be "encouraged" ([15 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/techsverige.pdf)). It also wants age checks, where needed, to use "interoperable" and privacy-protecting solutions. Interoperable means systems from different makers can work together. Its call for interoperability is about age checks, not parental controls, but it is close to Handrail's argument. TechSverige should be one of the first industry meetings.
- **Google** (the response is from Google Ireland on Google Sverige's letterhead) opposes a separate Swedish law. It asks Sweden to pause work on the bill and give priority to the KIDS Act and the DSA. It reads the KIDS Act as allowing limited accounts controlled by a guardian from age 13 (Articles 6 and 7) ([16 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/google-sverige.pdf)). **TikTok**, writing about age checks, supports privacy-preserving solutions including "age signals at operating system level and industry standards" ([16 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/tiktok.pdf)). Both are openings for one question: would you accept a certified plan from a controller you did not build?
- **Games.** A secondary report of Dataspelsbranschen's 2025 industry report gives 1,101 Swedish games companies and revenue of SEK 37 billion for 2024, with SEK 36.8 billion labelled "domestic" ([Nordic Game, 26 November 2025](https://nordicgame.com/new-swedish-games-industry-report-reveals-growth/), a secondary report). Its response to the consultation is not yet published. Folkhälsomyndigheten wants gaming platforms brought under the limit. That gives game makers a reason to prefer a plan-based route. Ask Dataspelsbranschen for one member studio with a large child audience to join the pilot as a test service.
- **Telecom operators.** Operators reach the home router, which is one way to apply a plan to devices that have no parental app. Telia already partners with Bris. I did not confirm which trade body speaks for operators on this topic.

## Standards bodies

The strongest finding of this research is in an ETSI report. ETSI is the European standards body for telecoms. In February 2025 its Human Factors committee published a proposed roadmap for age verification standards. Of the standards bodies (SDOs) it examined, it found that "no SDO has been explicitly identified that addresses Parental Control". It adds that many access control provisions still apply. It recommends updating ETSI TS 102 165-2, a security specification that includes a general model for access control, "to explicitly address the mechanisms for parental, or third party, control" ([ETSI TR 104 077-3, February 2025](https://etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/104000_104099/10407703/01.01.01_60/tr_10407703v010101p.pdf)). The report notes that an update to TS 102 165-2 was in development in ETSI's CYBER committee, planned for mid-2025. Handrail is a candidate answer to that gap.

The DSA itself asks the Commission to support voluntary standards, including "standards for targeted measures to protect minors online" (Article 44(1)(j); [text as reproduced by an unofficial DSA site](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_44.html); I could not open EUR-Lex, so check the official text there). I found no evidence that such a standard has been started.

- **CEN and CENELEC** (the general European standards bodies) published CWA 18016, an "age appropriate digital services framework", in September 2023. A CWA (Workshop Agreement) is a quick, light standard, not a full European Norm. CEN-CENELEC said "the next step should be" to develop it into a European standard ([CEN-CENELEC, 14 September 2023](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2023/eninthespotlight/2023-09-14-cwa-18016-children-protection-online/)). I found no confirmed follow-up.
- **SIS** (Swedish Institute for Standards) is appointed by the government as Sweden's standards body for ISO and CEN work ([SIS](https://www.sis.se/om-sis/)). I found no SIS committee on children's digital services. Ask SIS whether a Swedish proposal could start a new work item at CEN, and what joining would cost.
- **W3C** remains the right first home for the draft (see the main paper). Using W3C and an ETSI or CEN route together is common. The W3C draft proves the idea in public. The European body gives it a formal status that regulators can cite.

## EU bodies and networks

- **The Commission's digital department, DG CNECT,** manages the BIK website ([BIK, About](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/about)). The Commission supervises the largest platforms, so much of the supervision of the Swedish limit would fall to the Commission or to the authority in the country where a platform is based ([Mediemyndigheten response, 12 October 2026](https://www.regeringen.se/contentassets/9749352ad9724cd89625ad26807ea933/mediemyndigheten.pdf)).
- **The European Board for Digital Services** is where national coordinators meet the Commission. Its Working Group 6 covers protection of minors: the Article 28 guidelines, a task force on age verification, adult content and media literacy ([Commission, Board working groups, updated 2 July 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-board-working-groups)). On 15 April 2026 the Board said the protection of minors online "remains a top priority". It committed to using the guidelines as a benchmark to assess platforms' compliance with Article 28(1) ([Commission, 15 April 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/press-statement-european-board-digital-services)). Sweden is heard there through PTS, with Mediemyndigheten on Article 28.
- **BIK and Insafe.** BIK (Better Internet for Kids) is the EU's initiative and website for children online. Insafe and INHOPE work together through a network of Safer Internet Centres. Each centre is typically co-funded by the EU and has an awareness centre, a helpline, a hotline and a youth panel ([BIK, About](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/about)). **SIC Sweden** is coordinated by Mediemyndigheten and run with Bris and ECPAT. Its last reported EU co-funded project period ran from 1 January 2025 to 30 June 2026 ([Mediemyndigheten, SIC Sweden project report, 2026](https://mediemyndigheten.se/globalassets/rapporter-och-analyser/2026/projektrapport-safer-internet-centre-sverige-2025-2026.pdf)). I could not confirm how it is funded after June 2026. "Check the game", a site where parents and professionals can look up games their children want to play, is a natural place to explain plans ([BIK, 16 May 2025](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/news/spotlight-safer-internet-centres-norway-and-sweden)).

## Nordic cooperation

- **Nordic Welfare Centre** (an institution within the Nordic Council of Ministers) leads "Safer Digital Democracy", from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2026. It is to produce a Nordic research analysis, a policy overview, a report of children's and young people's views, and **joint Nordic recommendations** ([Nordic Welfare Centre, project page](https://nordicwelfare.org/en/projekt/safer-digital-democracy-2)). No outputs were listed when I read the page. This is a closing window. Input sent in November could still reach the recommendations.
- **The Nordic SIC network** is an informal network linking the centres in Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden. Finland chairs it in 2026 ([BIK, July 2026](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/sic/sweden)).
- The Nordic Council's own pages (norden.org) blocked my requests, so I could not check the 2025 and 2026 Council decisions directly.

**What this means for the 3.5 million EUR.** No single programme found here fits the steward, the reference software and the pilot together. The realistic mix has three parts. A government line, through Socialdepartementet or an agency task, pays for the pilot and evaluation. Small grants such as Innovativa Startups pay for the company. The open-source parts join an EU consortium when a fitting call appears. The steward's 2027 budget of 300,000 EUR still has no named source. That remains the main paper's honest weakness.

## Open questions

1. How is SIC Sweden funded after 30 June 2026? Will it apply under, or was it covered by, the 2025 or 2026 DIGITAL calls?
2. Has ETSI updated TS 102 165-2 as its 2025 roadmap recommended? The roadmap says an update was under way in ETSI's CYBER committee; did it cover parental control?
3. Has the Commission begun any work on DSA Article 44(1)(j) standards for minors?
4. Will Dataspelsbranschen, Meta and Snap publish consultation responses, and what do they say about parental controls?
5. Does Internetstiftelsen still make grants in any form?
6. Is there a Horizon Europe or Digital Europe topic in 2027 that would fund the open-source controller or the pilot?
7. Will the Nordic Welfare Centre's joint recommendations be published before the end of 2026?
8. Which body speaks for Swedish telecom operators on child safety at home?
