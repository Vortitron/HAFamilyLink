# Kerb diagrams

Eight diagram specifications for the proposal "Kerb: a floor and a path". Each one lists the exact boxes, arrows and labels so a designer can draw it without reading the proposal. Style notes for all eight: plain line drawing, two tones plus one accent, sans-serif labels, no logos of real companies (use the text name in a plain box), no photographs, no real children. UK English throughout. Where a number appears it is taken from the proposal and should be kept as written. Every household count is illustrative, year three, and is labelled so.

---

## D1. Two instruments side by side

**Caption.** A minimum-age ban and a Kerb household plan are both compliance instruments. One checks everyone and teaches nothing. The other checks no child's identity and teaches for ten years. Kerb is offered beside the ban, not instead of it.

**Layout.** Two equal columns, a thin vertical rule between them, a four-row footer table spanning both columns.

**Left column, header: "Minimum-age ban".**
- Top: a brick wall, full width of the column, labelled "16".
- Above the wall, a crowd of simple person icons labelled "Everyone, adults included". An arrow from the crowd down into a box labelled "ID check, selfie or card". Arrow from that box to the wall.
- A curved arrow that leaves the crowd, bypasses the wall around its right end, labelled "false birthdate, borrowed account, VPN".
- At the base of the wall on the far side, a small cliff edge with a child icon standing at the top, labelled "16th birthday: full access, no preparation".
- Small callout bottom left: "81% of 10 to 15 year olds still using, measured three months in (eSafety, July 2026)".

**Right column, header: "Kerb household plan".**
- Bottom left: a low kerb stone (a step, not a wall), labelled "the floor: statutory default profile, published by the regulator".
- From the kerb, a path rises to the top right in six treads. Tread labels in order: "Stopping", "Choosing", "Meeting", "Noticing", "Judging", "Owning".
- A child icon on the third tread. Two adult icons and a small speech-bubble icon labelled "guide" standing beside the path, not on it.
- At the top of the path, three plain boxes labelled "OS", "Console", "Social service", each with a small circular badge labelled "Kerb mark".
- A dashed horizontal line at the height of the first tread, running across the whole column, labelled "a child with no plan stays here".
- Small callout bottom right: "No field for name, date of birth, ID, competence or reasons. One pairwise id per platform."

**Footer table, four rows, two columns.** Row labels at far left: "Privacy cost", "Evasion", "Learning", "Auditability". Left cells: "All adults prove age", "High, half never asked", "None until 16", "Account counts". Right cells: "None for managed accounts, a plan not a passport", "Measured in pilot", "Six milestones", "Public registry, read-back, synthetic audit".

## D2. The path, with the floor

**Caption.** One child from first tablet to independence. Rules move from parent to child as competence is shown, and what parents can see narrows as the child grows. The statutory floor applies automatically to any account that carries no plan, so nothing here depends on every parent engaging.

**Layout.** Landscape. A horizontal timeline across the middle.

**Timeline.** Axis from "4" at left to "16" at right, with ticks at 4, 7, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16. Label beneath the axis in small type: "Ages shown are one child's path, not a schedule. Milestones carry no ages."

**Above the axis, six milestone flags in order, positioned loosely over the ticks, with no ages in the labels:** "Stopping", "Choosing", "Meeting", "Noticing", "Judging", "Owning". Under each flag one observable behaviour in smaller type: "stops at the limit without a lock", "can say what she made this week", "can say what she would do if a stranger messaged her", "can explain why a feed keeps going", "can tell an advert from a friend", "chose her own bedtime and kept it".

**Below the axis, two stacked bands the full width of the timeline, each of constant height, two shades.**
- Upper band, "Who sets the rules". The darker share is labelled "rules set by parents" and fills almost the whole band at age 4, shrinking to a sliver at 16. The lighter share is labelled "rules set by the child" and grows to fill almost the whole band at 16. A single thin horizontal line inside the band, constant height, labelled "bedtime: protected first, handed over last", which only switches shade at 15.
- Lower band, "What parents can see". The darker share is labelled "per-app detail, shared with the child" and runs from 4 to the Noticing flag. At Noticing it steps down to a thin strip labelled "totals against budget and whether each rule landed", which runs to 16. Above the step, small type: "household sets the milestone; default Noticing".

**Three speech bubbles from the guide, drawn above the flags, each with a parent reply beneath it:**
1. At 7: bubble "Tagged creative and offline-ok by 312 households (illustrative). Has in-app purchases." Reply: "Allow. Purchases need approval. Count half."
2. At 13: bubble "Starter policy for a first social account: private, no endless feed, no notifications after 20:00, 60 minutes." Reply: "Send it. She can read every rule."
3. At 14: bubble, pointing at the child icon rather than the parents: "Three late nights in a row. Move your own cut-off earlier?" Reply from the child: "OK, 21:30." Small type beside it: "her parents see totals only from here".

**Below the bands, a dashed horizontal line labelled "the floor: statutory default profile, applied to any account with no plan".** A faint vertical dotted line at 16 labelled "where a ban alone would start".

## D3. Actors and the home boundary

**Caption.** The whole system. Everything about a child lives inside the home boundary. Only the effects of a plan cross it to a platform, under an identifier unique to that platform, and only app identifiers and anonymous tag votes ever go to the community service.

**Layout.** A large rounded rectangle on the left labelled "Home". Platforms on the right. Community service above right. Steward and regulator below right.

**Inside "Home":**
- Person icon "Child", with an eye icon and label "sees the whole ledger; after Noticing, sees per-app detail alone".
- Two person icons "Guardians".
- A box "Household controller (certified)" containing three smaller boxes: "Policy", "Raw ledger (31 days, then deleted)" and "Summary (milestone names and dates only)".
- A box "Guide (AI, certified)", drawn inside the home, connected only to "Household controller" by a double-headed arrow labelled "reads ledger and tags, proposes, never decides". A small note: "runs here by default; if any part is remote it gets a minimal view, no retention, no training".
- A list in the corner headed "Never leaves this box:" with items "name, date of birth, location beyond country, credentials, content, messages, device ids, per-app usage, milestones, reasons, who made a decision".

**Right of the boundary, four plain boxes stacked:** "Operating system", "Console", "Game store", "Social service". Each has a small badge "Kerb mark, Level n" (write "Level 1", "Level 3", "Level 2", "Level 2" respectively, as examples). Each box carries a different short random string labelled once: "pairwise child id, different for every platform".

**Arrows from "Household controller" crossing the boundary to each platform box:** one solid arrow labelled "policy effects (age band, country, budget to 15 min, windows to 30 min, bedtime, rules by app id and rating; tags resolved at home)" and one solid arrow labelled "override (effect, scope, expiry; no reason, no actor)". **Return arrows from each platform:** "result: applied, confirmed, partial, failed, unknown", "usage facts: device, app, minutes", "child request: app id + type, no text".

**Above right, a cloud labelled "Community service (open data)".** One thin dashed arrow from the home to the cloud labelled "app id + tag vote, opt-in only, one anonymous token per vote, via relay". One solid arrow back labelled "tags and named profiles". A note beside the cloud: "holds nothing about any child; a developer may dispute a tag".

**Below right, two boxes:** "Steward (non-profit)" and "Regulator". Arrow from Steward to the platform boxes labelled "certification mark, annual re-test, notice, cure, appeal, delisting". Arrow from Steward to the platform boxes, thin, labelled "synthetic test accounts (the only field audit)". One arrow from Steward to "Household controller", crossing the boundary, labelled "controller credential, chained from the OS family account, revocable; carries no household data back". Arrow from Regulator to Steward labelled "audits". Arrow from Regulator to the platform boxes labelled "recognises the mark, may require it". Arrow from Regulator to a small box beside the registry labelled "publishes the statutory default profile, versioned, by jurisdiction". No other arrow between Steward or Regulator and the home.

## D4. The honest result loop

**Caption.** Why "unknown is never unlocked" is the heart of trust. One override, from request to expiry, with the read-back step that no platform may skip.

**Layout.** A state diagram, left to right, rounded boxes for states, arrows with labels.

**States, in order:** "Requested", "Applied", "Confirmed", "Expired". Branches from "Applied" downward to "Partial", "Failed", "Unknown". A separate state "Cancelled" below the main row, reachable from any state by a thin arrow labelled "compensating unlock".

**Arrows and labels:**
- Requested to Applied: "writes sent to each platform".
- Applied to Confirmed: "every platform read back its own state and it matched".
- Applied to Partial: "some read back, some did not".
- Applied to Failed: "none read back within the window".
- Applied to Unknown: "platform cannot read its own state".
- Confirmed or Partial to Expired: "lifetime ends, default end of day".

**A box to the right of "Unknown" labelled "What the household sees, the same on both screens", containing three lines:** "Rule in force: locked.", "Tablet has not confirmed since 14:02.", "Enforcement health: amber". Small type: "child and parent see the same three lines".

**A struck-through alternative arrow** from "Unknown" to a greyed box labelled "Child shown as free", with a bold red cross over the arrow and the caption "conformance violation; counts toward the delisting threshold (notice, cure, appeal)".

**Footer in bold:** "A failed lock is still a decision in force. Both are told. Unknown is never shown as unlocked."

**Example strip at the very bottom, three short lines:** "Phone: locked, confirmed 17:01." "Console: allowance zeroed, confirmed 17:01." "Second console: not participating, network control only (grey)."

## D5. Community knowledge flow

**Caption.** How parents' knowledge about apps becomes shared tags and profiles without any child or household being identified, and why the local plan always wins.

**Layout.** Bottom to top, with a side loop.

**Bottom row:** twelve small house icons. From each, one tiny token rises labelled "app id + tag + up/down" with a small random string beside it (for example "c7f3", "a91e") labelled once: "one anonymous token per vote, issued blind by the steward per app, tag and epoch; not a household id; sent via relay".

**Middle: a box "Curated vocabulary, about 30 tags" divided into five columns:** "Value: educational, creative, reading", "Social: friends-only, chat-with-strangers, voice-chat", "Commerce: in-app purchases, loot boxes", "Content: mild violence, horror", "Attention: endless feed, autoplay, no stopping point, offline-ok".

**Four gates drawn as turnstiles between the tokens and the vocabulary box, labelled:** "net score positive to show", "caution tags need evidence and three contributors", "maximum ten visible tags per app", "developer dispute: tag suppressed pending steward review".

**Upper right: a shelf labelled "Named profiles"** with three cards: "Strict under 10 (2,140 households)", "Educational first (1,320 households)", "Swedish health guidance (890 households)". A label on the shelf: "counts illustrative, year three; day one shows 'curated' instead". Arrows from the shelf back down to the house icons labelled "subscribe".

**Left side: one house icon enlarged,** with a bold arrow straight into its own box "This house's plan", bypassing the vocabulary box entirely, labelled "local plan always wins: 'Minecraft counts as educational here' is one record". A second thin arrow from the vocabulary box into this house, labelled "tags resolved to app ids here; platforms never receive a tag".

**Top: a small box "Guide"** taking three thin arrows in (from the vocabulary box, from the profile shelf, from "This house's plan") and one speech bubble out: "14 households in your profile tag this game chat-with-strangers. Allow with chat off?" A second, smaller bubble: "No votes yet on this app. Curated tags only."

**Right edge: a disk icon** labelled "nightly dump, open data, works offline" with a shield icon beneath labelled "no names, no usage, no children, no households".

## D6. Certification ladder and the IARC pattern

**Caption.** Five conformance levels, who is certified at each, what households observe today, and how one certified result is recognised in many jurisdictions.

**Layout.** Left: a ladder. Centre: the test and registry. Right: the recognition fan-out. Bottom left: a separate box about today.

**Ladder, five rungs bottom to top, each with a one-line requirement to its right:**
- "Level 0, Observer: reports minutes used."
- "Level 1, Cut-off: can stop now, release later, and confirm it."
- "Level 2, Budget: accepts a daily allowance and windows, confirms by read-back."
- "Level 3, Interactive: Level 2 plus grants, child requests, per-app rules."
- "Level 4, Native: accepts policy and override objects at one endpoint, emits standard events."

**Markers on the rungs, as of 2027:** Level 0 and Level 1: "household routers, smart plugs and the reference controller". Levels 2, 3 and 4: dotted outline with the text "nobody yet". No vendor names appear on the ladder.

**Separate box, bottom left, headed "Today, as observed by households. Not certified. No vendor has applied."** Plain text, no hazard symbols: "Most consoles and phones: native parental controls that a household can set in the vendor's own app, with no read-back. Some PC family services: budgets and windows the household can set by hand. Network and plug controls: cut-off only. No vendor exposes a parental-control interface." A note: "Kerb grades only those who apply."

**Centre: two boxes connected by an arrow.** "Self-service conformance suite (runs on a laptop)" then "Public registry". Inside the registry box two sample rows: "Example TV OS | Level 2 | certified 2028-09 | suite 1.x | status: listed" and "Default profile, UK | version 2 | published by regulator 2028-04". Beneath: "annual re-test against the suite version on record; minor versions never invalidate a listing; notice, 90-day cure, independent appeal, then delisting, prospective only".

**Right: a fan of six plain boxes labelled with jurisdictions** ("UK Ofcom", "Australia eSafety", "EU Commission", "Greece", "Denmark", "Sweden") each with an arrow pointing to the single registry box, every arrow labelled "recognises the mark". Beneath the Australia box in small type: "path requires an amendment to primary legislation".

**Inset, bottom right, small:** "The IARC pattern: one questionnaire, nine rating authorities" drawn as one form feeding five small badge outlines labelled "ESRB", "PEGI", "USK", "ClassInd", "ACB" with the caption "five of nine shown". Note beneath: "Kerb sits beside ISO/IEC 27566-1; field names mirror Matter Content Control where they match."

## D7. Who pays, by stage

**Caption.** The steward's funding over three stages, borrowed from standards that worked, with the two-implementation gate that decides whether the model closes and the honest arithmetic of when dues can carry it.

**Layout.** Three ascending steps left to right, a rising cost line, a decision diamond, and a footnote row.

**Step 1 (2027), label block:** "W3C Community Group. Cost to join: zero. Funded by: named government pilot sponsor; one or two foundation sponsors. Delivers: spec 1.0, conformance suite, reference controller, pilot."

**Step 2 (2028), label block:** "Non-profit steward, ISRG model. Funded by: sponsors. Delivers: registry live, credentials, first certified platform, pilot results."

**Step 3 (2029 onward), label block:** "Membership dues, CSA scale 7,500 to 112,500 USD by tier; certification about 2,000 EUR per product; free tier for non-profits, academics, small developers. Sponsor line continues."

**A cost line across all three steps, stepping up:** "about 300,000 EUR a year" over Step 1, "about 600,000 EUR a year" over Steps 2 and 3. **Beneath Step 3, a small table headed "When do dues cover it?":** "2 members, mid tier: under 50,000 USD", "10 members mixed: about 250,000 USD", "25 members mixed: about 600,000 EUR, earliest 2031". A bracket under the sponsor line reading "government and sponsors carry the gap until then".

**A diamond at the end of Step 2 labelled "two independent implementations conform? (reference controller plus first platform)"** Arrow "yes" leads up to Step 3. Arrow "no" leads to a flat box at the right labelled "specification stays open in the Community Group; households keep what they have".

**Two small boxes beneath the steps:** "The steward never charges parents or children" and "The steward never sells data; it holds nothing about any child". A third, smaller box: "The company sells a certified controller and guide to households; see FUNDING-LANDSCAPE.md".

**Footnote row at the bottom, three broken-wheel icons with labels:** "Do Not Track: voluntary, no enforcement, closed 2019", "P3P: no client reader, obsoleted 2018", "ICRA labels: publishers self-label, shut 2010". Beside them one intact wheel labelled "Global Privacy Control: statute made it a duty, 2022 onward".

## D8. The minister's calendar

**Caption.** The UK ban commences spring 2027. The first certified platform cannot exist before 2028. These three asks fit between the two dates and commit the government to nothing until a mark exists.

**Layout.** A Gantt strip over 2027, 2028 and 2029, with three highlighted rows at the top and the roadmap rows beneath.

**Top three rows, accent colour, each with a diamond at its date:**
- "Clause in secondary legislation: 'a certified graduated household plan may meet the minimum-age duty', with safe harbour and acceptance of the OS parent-declared band" diamond at Q1 2027, aligned with a vertical dashed line labelled "ban commences".
- "Pilot sponsorship signed (about 3.5 million EUR); pre-registered evaluation design published" diamond at Q2 2027.
- "Twelve-month review: pilot outcomes reported beside ban outcomes" diamond at Q2 2028.

**Roadmap rows beneath, plain colour, drawn as bars:**
- "Kerb 1.0 specification and conformance suite" 2027 Q1 to Q3.
- "Reference household controller (household devices and allow-listed network controls)" 2027 Q1 to Q4.
- "Steward curates first 200 to 500 apps against the vocabulary" 2027 Q2 to Q4.
- "Pilot: 400 households across income bands, university as data controller" 2027 Q3 to 2028 Q3.
- "Community service live, curated tags plus IARC ratings" 2027 Q4 to 2028 Q2.
- "First platform at Level 2" bar 2028 Q1 to Q3, diamond at Q3 "first certified platform".
- "Steward incorporated" diamond at 2028 Q4, with a small gate symbol "two implementations: reference controller plus first platform".
- "Regulator cites the mark in guidance" diamond at 2029 Q3, drawn in outline with the label "the lever every successful standard needed; widens the moat, not the start of revenue".
- "Second platform certified; membership opens" diamond at 2029 Q4.
- "Outcome report against ban baseline" diamond at 2029 Q4.

**A thin row at the bottom labelled "evidence":** a bar beginning 2028 Q1 labelled "consented, minimal, longitudinal under-18 data, held by the university: sleep, wellbeing, media literacy, parent-child conflict, six milestones".

**Footer:** "If the 2029 diamond is missed, the standard stays open and the households keep what they have."
